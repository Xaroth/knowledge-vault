# Why

I am a senior individual contributor **aiming for a lead engineer promotion**. I
want two things at once, because a promotion into a lead role requires both:

* **Skills** — to actually operate as a technical leader, not just hold the title.
* **Evidence** — a track record of leadership-level impact that makes the
  promotion an obvious recognition of what I already do, rather than a bet.

The strongest way to get promoted into a lead role is to start doing the job
before you have the title: operate one scope up, visibly, until the promotion is a
formality. This mission exists to make that deliberate rather than accidental.

# What "lead engineer" means here

Across the industry the level above *senior* has many names — staff, lead, tech
lead, principal — but the common thread is a **shift in scope of impact**: from
being responsible for your own work to being responsible for the technical success
of a team and the things around it. This track uses the four
[Staff archetypes](./glossary.md) (Tech Lead, Architect, Solver, Right Hand) as a
map of the shapes that role can take.

# Scope — the four dimensions

All four dimensions are in scope, because a lead is expected across all of them:

1. **Technical direction** — architecture decisions, tech strategy, setting
   standards, managing tech debt, RFCs/ADRs.
2. **People & mentoring** — growing engineers, feedback, delegation, code review
   as teaching, 1:1s.
3. **Delivery & execution** — breaking down work, estimation, unblocking, shipping,
   managing scope and risk.
4. **Influence & communication** — cross-team alignment, upward communication,
   writing, running meetings and decisions.

# What success looks like

* I can name the specific behaviours a lead demonstrates in each dimension, and
  point to times I have demonstrated them.
* I am consistently operating one scope up: my impact is visible beyond my own
  tickets.
* I have a concrete, evidenced case for promotion that my manager and skip-level
  would recognise.

# Working assumptions (confirm or correct)

* The target level is a **lead engineer** role at my current
  employer, on a technical (not people-management) track.
* No fixed deadline yet; the aim is steady, evidenced growth toward the next
  promotion cycle.
* My current day-to-day is hands-on software engineering, so lessons should connect
  leadership ideas back to concrete engineering work rather than abstract theory.
* I am a self-taught engineer. I have some formal training in software engineering, but most of my skills have been learned on the job.
  I am comfortable with self-directed learning and reflection.
* My company operates within the games industry, I am part of a small team aimed at shipping a single product, and I am part of a slightly
  larger team aimed at shipping a single product that affects multiple other products. I am not a manager, but I have some influence over the work of others.
* My segment is a cross-product segment (websites, launcher, API), so our workload is dependent on the work of other teams, and we have to
  often coordinate with them to ship our work.
* My segment currently has a lead engineer, however, I feel that I am better suited for the role, and I want to demonstrate that I can operate
  at that level. This also means that there might be some friction between me and the current lead engineer, and I want to be able to navigate

  relationship while also demonstrating my capabilities.
