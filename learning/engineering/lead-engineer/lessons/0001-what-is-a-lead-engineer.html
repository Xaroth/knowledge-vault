Lead Engineer · Lesson 1 · The role

What a lead engineer actually is

It's not a bigger version of "senior." It's a wider one.

You are aiming for a lead promotion. Before you can operate one scope up on purpose, you need a clear picture of the target. This lesson gives you that picture in two ideas — scope of impact and the four archetypes — and asks you to locate yourself on the map. That's the win: by the end you can say, in one sentence, what the job is and which shape of it you're closest to.

Idea 1 — Level is scope of impact, not seniority

The jump from senior to lead is not "write more code" or "be more senior." It is a change in scope of impact: what your work is responsible for. A senior engineer is responsible for their own output. A lead is responsible for the technical success of a team and the things around it — decisions, standards, other people's effectiveness.1

This is why more years, or deeper expertise in one stack, doesn't automatically make you a lead. You grow by widening scope, not only by going deeper. Your main output stops being code and starts being leverage — you become a multiplier of the engineers around you.2

Why this matters for your promotion Promotion committees look for evidence that you are already operating at the wider scope. "Operating one scope up" before you hold the title is the whole strategy — see your mission.

Idea 2 — The four archetypes

"Lead" takes different shapes. Will Larson found that almost every senior technical leader fits one of four archetypes.1 They're a map, not a cage — most people are mainly one, with traces of others.

Tech Lead

Guides one team's approach and execution, usually paired with its manager. The most common on-ramp from senior IC.

Architect

Owns a critical area's direction, quality, and approach across a multi-year horizon.

Solver

Drops into arbitrarily hard problems and finds a path forward; goes deep or roams between hotspots.

Right Hand

Extends a senior executive's scope and authority to lead a large organisation. The rarest, most senior shape.

Full definitions live in the glossary. For someone aiming for a first lead role, Tech Lead is almost always the on-ramp — hold that in mind as you self-assess below.

Do this before Lesson 2

One small real-world action to convert this into evidence: write two or three sentences naming which archetype your current work is closest to, and one concrete thing you already do that fits the wider scope (not just your own tickets).
