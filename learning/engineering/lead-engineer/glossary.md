The canonical vocabulary for this track. Every lesson adheres to these
definitions; when a term is used in a lesson it links back here.

# Core

**Scope of impact** — The blast radius of your work: whether you are responsible
for your own tasks, your team's technical success, or outcomes spanning many
teams. Level (senior → lead → staff → principal) tracks scope of impact far more
than years of experience. Growing means *widening scope*, not just going deeper.

**Individual contributor (IC)** — An engineer on the technical track (no direct
reports). Lead and staff are senior IC levels: leadership without management.

**Lead engineer** — Used in this track as an umbrella for the first level whose
scope of impact is a *team's* technical success rather than one person's output.
Overlaps with "tech lead" and "staff engineer" depending on the organisation.

# The four Staff archetypes

Will Larson's map of the shapes a Staff+ / lead role takes. Most people are
primarily one, with elements of others.

**Tech Lead** — Guides the approach and execution of *one team* (sometimes two or
three), usually paired with an engineering manager. The most common on-ramp to
leadership from senior IC.

**Architect** — Owns the direction, quality, and approach of a *critical area*
across a multi-year horizon; combines deep technical knowledge with org-level
leadership.

**Solver** — Drops into arbitrarily complex or high-priority problems and finds a
path forward; either goes deep in one area or moves hotspot to hotspot.

**Right Hand** — Acts as an extension of a senior executive, borrowing their scope
and authority to lead a large organisation. The rarest and most senior archetype.

# Ways of working

**Leadership without authority** — Getting outcomes through influence, trust, and
technical credibility rather than positional power. The defining skill of a senior
IC leader, since they lead people who don't report to them.

**Glue work** — The unglamorous, often-invisible coordination that holds a project
together: unblocking others, writing the doc, chasing the decision, onboarding.
Essential to leadership, but risky if it fully replaces visible technical work —
name it and make it count rather than letting it go unrecognised.

**Operating one scope up** — Deliberately doing the job of the next level before
holding the title, so that a promotion recognises existing impact rather than
betting on potential. The core promotion strategy for this mission.

**Multiplier** — Someone whose main impact is making the engineers around them more
effective (via standards, mentoring, unblocking, better decisions) rather than
their own direct output. Being a multiplier is the point of the lead role.

# Decision & direction artifacts

**ADR (Architecture Decision Record)** — A short, dated document capturing one
architectural decision, its context, and its consequences. The durable memory of
*why* a system is shaped the way it is.

**RFC (Request for Comments)** — A proposal circulated for feedback before a
significant technical decision; the main tool for building alignment and leading
technical direction in writing.

**Tech strategy** — A small set of deliberate, hard-to-reverse choices about how a
team or org will approach its technical problems, and the reasoning behind them —
as opposed to a list of projects.
